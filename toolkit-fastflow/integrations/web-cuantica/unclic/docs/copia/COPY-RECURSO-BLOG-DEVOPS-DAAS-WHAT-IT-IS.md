## Introducción

DevOps has become essential for accelerating software delivery and improving quality. Yet implementing DevOps in-house can be challenging for many organizations. **DevOps as a Service (DaaS)** is a model that makes DevOps capabilities available on-demand as a managed service. In this guide we explore what DaaS is, how it differs from traditional DevOps, key benefits, who benefits most, how platforms and specialized providers fit in, and why managed CI/CD is a practical first step—without overpromising 24/7 or full-stack observability.

---

## What Is DevOps as a Service (DaaS)?

**DevOps as a Service** means accessing DevOps tools, processes, and expertise without building and maintaining everything in-house. As with Software-as-a-Service, you consume a ready-to-use capability: in this case, **CI/CD pipelines, automation, and deployment** delivered as a service. That can mean an external team or platform handling integration, testing, delivery, and infrastructure automation for you.

**Traditional DevOps** usually involves your own developers and IT operations building and running pipelines internally. **DaaS** shifts much of that collaboration and tooling to the cloud or a third-party provider. You still get continuous integration, delivery, and automation—but you don’t have to hire a full DevOps team or maintain complex in-house infrastructure. In short: *traditional DevOps = you build and run it; DaaS = you consume DevOps capabilities as a service.*

---

## DevOps as a Managed Service

“DevOps as a managed service” is often used interchangeably with DaaS. It means a **third party manages the DevOps environment** on your behalf: implementing CI/CD pipelines, automating infrastructure where applicable, and keeping delivery running. You focus on your product; the provider supplies the pipeline and know-how.

Control can vary: some providers offer **fully managed** (end-to-end), others a **platform** your team uses, and others a **modular service** (e.g. managed CI/CD and deploy only) with clear scope. The important point: DevOps becomes something you **subscribe to or contract**, rather than solely an internal effort.

---

## DevOps Platform as a Service vs. DaaS

- **DevOps platform as a service:** Cloud-based platforms that provide an integrated set of DevOps tools (source control, CI/CD, testing, deployment) as a subscription. Examples: Azure DevOps Services, GitLab, GitHub, AWS (CodePipeline, CodeBuild, CodeDeploy). They give you the **technology**; your team (or a partner) operates it.

- **DevOps as a Service (managed):** Often adds **expertise and ongoing management** on top of a platform. A company might use AWS or Azure for the toolchain and engage a **specialized DaaS provider** to design, configure, and maintain pipelines—so you get both the platform and the operational know-how.

For many organizations, the practical path is **managed CI/CD**: a provider runs the pipeline (e.g. Jenkins, Gitea, registry, deploy) with **scope agreed in writing**, without claiming full-stack observability or 24/7 support unless explicitly contracted.

## Key Benefits of DevOps as a Service (that we can honestly claim)

- **Access to expertise:** Get experienced pipeline and deployment practices without hiring and training a full in-house team.  
- **Faster setup and time-to-market:** A managed pipeline can be up and running in a fraction of the time it takes to build one from scratch.  
- **Cost efficiency:** Subscription or sprint-based pricing can be lower than a full-time DevOps team and large one-off consultancy projects.  
- **Scalability and flexibility:** Scale pipelines and environments (more repos, more stages) as needed; pay for what you use.  
- **Focus on core product:** Your team spends time on features and product, not on maintaining Jenkins servers or debugging deployment scripts.  
- **Clear scope:** With **scope and revisions defined in writing**, you avoid scope creep and know exactly what’s included (and what isn’t, e.g. no 24/7, no full observability unless agreed).

---

## Who Benefits Most from the DaaS Model?

- **Startups and SMBs:** Often can’t afford a full DevOps team; DaaS lets them adopt CI/CD and pipeline-as-code without huge upfront investment.  
- **Growing companies:** When release volume and complexity grow, a managed pipeline can prevent deployment from becoming a bottleneck.  
- **Teams without in-house DevOps expertise:** Partnering with a provider is faster than recruiting and training.  
- **Teams under time pressure:** Need CI/CD quickly for a launch or to fix a broken process; a provider can get pipelines running in days or weeks.  
- **Businesses that want to focus on product:** If managing servers and CI systems isn’t your differentiator, outsourcing that to a specialist makes sense.

*Any organization that wants the outcomes of DevOps (faster, more reliable releases) but struggles with implementation can benefit from DevOps as a Service—especially managed CI/CD with clear scope.*

## Leading DevOps as a Service Providers and Platforms

The landscape includes:

- **Cloud platforms (tooling):** AWS (CodePipeline, CodeBuild, CodeDeploy), Microsoft Azure DevOps, Google Cloud (Cloud Build, Cloud Deploy). They provide the technology; you or a partner operate it.  
- **Specialized managed providers:** Companies that offer **managed CI/CD and deployment**—designing and operating pipelines (e.g. Jenkins, Gitea, registry, deploy to EC2 or Kubernetes) with defined scope, subscriptions, or sprint-based engagement. This is where “managed CI/CD as a service” fits: you get pipeline-as-code and deployment without 24/7 or full observability unless explicitly agreed.

*Choosing a provider that is transparent about what’s included (and what’s not) helps set the right expectations.*

## Conclusion

DevOps as a Service is a practical way to adopt DevOps with less friction. By using **managed services and clear scope**, even smaller teams can get **automated, continuous delivery** without building everything in-house. Managed CI/CD—pipeline as code, Jenkins, Gitea, registry, deploy—delivers real value when the provider is transparent about what’s included (e.g. no 24/7, no full observability unless agreed) and when the engagement is defined in writing.

If your business is looking to embrace DevOps as a Service, choose a partner that is clear about scope, revisions, and deliverables. With the right approach, DaaS can streamline your workflows, reduce cost, and set you on the path to faster, more frequent releases.
